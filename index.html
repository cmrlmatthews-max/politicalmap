Method and limits

Party control score

  • The score is the average of five parts. Each part goes from −1 (Republican) to +1 (Democratic).
  • Part 1 is the 2024 presidential winner. Part 2 is the governor. Part 3 is the two US senators.
  • Part 4 is the share of US House seats. Part 5 is control of the two state legislative chambers.
  • Independent senators count with the party they vote with. A tied or coalition chamber counts as 0.
  • Nebraska has one nonpartisan chamber. It counts as Republican because Republicans hold most seats.

Correlation and adjustment

  • A correlation (r) goes from −1 to +1. A value near 0 means no link.
  • The adjusted value removes the part that income and urban population share explain. Income is median household income divided by the local price level.
  • A link is "clear" when chance alone gives it less than 1 time in 20. With 50 states, that is about ±0.28.
  • With this many tests, chance alone makes 1 or 2 links look clear.
  • Each state counts one time. Wyoming counts the same as California.

Limits

  • The score shows control today. Many statistics come from decades of policy, economy, and history.
  • The years are different for each statistic. See the table below.
  • Literacy data is from 2012 to 2017. Happiness rankings use weak methods. Both have low confidence.
  • Wyoming and Delaware have many company registrations from other states. Their business application rates are not typical.
  • Maternal mortality has no published rate for 11 small states.
  • US House seat counts are current to September 2, 2026.

Sources

Confidence shows how sure the data is: full square = primary source and checked, half square = secondary source or provisional, empty square = old or weak method. The arithmetic was run by code and checked.
